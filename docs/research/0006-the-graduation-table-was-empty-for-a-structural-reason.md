# 0006 — The graduation table was empty for a structural reason

**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Reproduce:** `radar graduations --store <store>`, and the CryptoHouse queries quoted below.

Radar reported a pump.fun graduation rate of **0.0133%** — four graduations in
30,574 recorded launches, every one of them completing within three slots of its
launch. That number was used to argue two things: that graduation is roughly 60×
rarer than the plan assumed, and that graduation is *inherently* an instant
event, so the `creator_edge` rule "prefer creators who have graduated a token"
selects for bundlers.

The first is wrong. The true rate is about **3%**, roughly three times the
plan's ~1% assumption rather than sixty times below it. The second is half right,
and only became measurable once the first was fixed.

Both followed from one line of mint resolution.

## What the rule was

`resolve_mint` takes the transaction's token-transfer rows, drops the quote
assets, and requires exactly one mint to remain. More than one is
`AmbiguousMint` and the row is skipped — deliberately, because an event
attributed to the wrong token is worse than a missing one.

That rule is right for a launch and wrong for a graduation, because the two
instructions do opposite things to the mints they touch.

A pump.fun migration moves **two** non-quote tokens: the subject token, and the
**LP mint the AMM pool creates in the same transaction**. So every migration had
two candidates, and every migration was refused.

Measured over one hour of chain (2026-08-23 22:00–23:00 UTC):

| Non-quote mints in the transaction | Migrations |
|---|---|
| 2 | 49 |
| 0 | 17 |
| **1** | **0** |

Not one migration in the sample could be recorded. The four rows in the store
were flukes, and — this is the part that mattered — they were a *biased* fluke
sample, because the degenerate migrations are the ones most likely to leave a
single resolvable mint. The store's evidence that "graduation is instant" was
generated by the same bug that made graduations invisible.

Launches were unaffected: 1,640 of 1,723 resolved cleanly, and the store's launch
rate (1,283/hr) matched the chain's (1,328/hr mean over three sampled hours).
Only the graduation path was broken, which is why nothing else looked wrong.

## The fix, and why it is structural rather than a heuristic

The two mints are distinguishable, and in the right direction:

- The **LP mint is created by the migration** — it appears as `MintTo` then
  `Burn`. It did not exist before this transaction.
- The **subject token predates it** and only moves (`TransferChecked`).

So the subject is the non-quote mint the transaction did not mint. This is a fact
about what the instruction does, not a pattern that happened to hold.

**It must not be applied to launches.** A `create` mints its own subject: 300 of
303 distinct mints in sampled launch transactions were minted in the transaction
itself. Inverting the rule across the board would have destroyed the working
half, so resolution is keyed on the instruction.

**The amount is not usable.** The subject's transfers summed to exactly
413,800,000,000,000 in one sample, which looks like the bonding-curve remainder
and is tempting. It is 206,900,000,000,000 in another. A constant would have
matched under half of them and looked principled while doing it.

**Where there are no transfer rows at all**, the transaction's
`post_token_balances` carry the mint. CryptoHouse has no `token_transfers` row
for about a fifth of successful migrations, and widening the query window does
not recover them — it is a coverage gap, not a partition edge.

Together the two sources resolved **62 of 62** successful migrations in the
sampled hour to exactly one mint.

## Two further corrections found by reading the rows

Neither was in the original diagnosis. Both were found by extracting the hour and
looking at what came out, which is the only reason they are here.

**A failed `migrate` is not a graduation.** 35 of the 97 migration instructions
in that hour were in failed transactions. They are worth recording — an attempted
and reverted migration is real information — but counting them as graduations
overstated the label by more than a third.

**A token can carry more than one successful migration instruction.** The 62
successful rows covered only **50 distinct mints**, several as a `migrate`
followed a slot later by a `migrate_v2`. The table is right to keep every event;
a token graduates once, so the count and the rate are over mints. Counting rows
overstated the rate by 24%.

## What the numbers are now

Re-extracting the same hour through the fixed path:

```
launches recorded : 1642
graduations       : 50
population rate   : 3.0450%  (50 of 1642)
  of which instant  : 14  (<= 3 slots from launch)
  of which organic  : 19
  duration unknown  : 17  (launched before this store started)

distinct creators who graduated a token: 31
creators who did it more than once:
  2oFadQnwBHLASJVVKWYHT2GrMmYLg5RUNTu2yZwQd2HD  2 graduations from 7 launches
  3yct19th5Cjpu8wMzDWpYVF32ESzo4gtrtTwiuWNBKo8  2 graduations from 23 launches
```

The last block is the one that changes what is possible. `radar graduations`
previously printed *"none of them did it twice, so 'has graduated before' has no
sample to predict from yet"*. In one hour of correctly extracted chain there are
31 creators with a graduation and several with two. The question the creator
signal exists to answer is now answerable, which it was not before.

## The split is real, and it is why the rule changed anyway

The original concern was correct even though its evidence was not. Over 44
graduations sampled independently of the store, slots from first transfer to
migration:

```
min 0   p10 0   median 828 (~5 min)   p90 274,163   max 809,491 (~3.4 days)
0 slots (same block):  12 / 44  (27%)
<= 900 slots (~5 min): 23 / 44  (52%)
```

Bimodal: a hard spike at zero, then a broad organic tail, with **nothing between
0 and 25 slots**. Nobody discovers a token, decides, and buys out its entire
bonding curve inside one block; capital that size arriving that fast was
committed before the token existed.

So the outcome label is split. `Outcome` now records `graduated_at` — the slot,
not a flag — and [`GraduationMode`](../../crates/radar-store/src/outcome.rs)
derives `Instant` from `Organic` at `INSTANT_WITHIN_SLOTS = 3`. The threshold
sits inside the observed gap rather than on a slope, so moving it anywhere
between 0 and 25 changes no answer.

`creator_edge` gates on the **organic** rate. A creator whose tokens only ever
graduate in their own launch block has a graduation rate and no evidence of
demand, and selecting on the undifferentiated rate would rank them top.

The names are measurements rather than verdicts: `Instant` says what was
observed. Calling it `Bundled` would assume the conclusion, the same way calling
a stillborn token a rug would.

## Deployed, and what the whole store says

Repaired on the live instance on 2026-08-24 — `--scope graduations` over the
store's own window recovered **1,740 graduation events at 97.5% yield**, with the
44 it could not resolve counted under `GraduationSubjectUnresolved` rather than
disappearing among skipped trades. Outcomes were re-measured against them.

```
launches recorded : 33932
graduations       : 1088
population rate   : 3.2064%  (1088 of 33932)
  of which instant  : 369  (<= 3 slots from launch)
  of which organic  : 566
  duration unknown  : 153  (launched before this store started)

distinct creators who graduated a token: 803
```

Four graduations became 1,088; one creator with a graduation became 803. Of the
935 with a measurable duration, **39% are instant** — close to the 27% and 42%
seen in the two independent samples, and far too large a share to leave folded
into one label.

The separation the signal needs is now visible. Against a 3.2% population rate,
measured creators run from `bwamJzzt…` at 2 graduations from **403** launches
(0.5%, below the population rate) to `DyyJbubC…` at 6 from 11 and `9Mztn56c…` at
8 from 26 — an order of magnitude either side of the base rate. Before the fix
every one of these creators had a rate of zero.

### The decision lane, before and after

The same command over the same instance, either side of the repair:

| `radar consider --window 6000` | before ([0005](0005-first-end-to-end-decision-pass.md)) | after |
|---|---|---|
| `CreatorNeverGraduated` | 1,570 — every creator | 158 |
| `CreatorGraduatesTooRarely` | *unreachable* | 27 |
| `InputsTooStale` | 2,014 | 0 |
| reached the paid tier | **0** | **32** |

`CreatorGraduatesTooRarely` could not fire before, because a threshold on a rate
whose numerator is always zero is dead code that looks like a working rule. The
paid tier had never run: 0005 reported *"spending on exit analysis would change
no answer, so nothing is spent"*, which read as the tiering working and was
actually the strategy short-circuiting. It now spends, and refuses on measured
grounds — `CapacityBelowFloor` and `NoRoute` — which is the first time those
refusals have been about the tokens rather than about the absence of data.

Nothing was proposed. That remains the correct outcome for a strategy whose
thresholds are untuned by design.

## What this does not establish

Whether organic graduation history predicts anything. There is now a sample where
there was none, which is a precondition for the question rather than an answer to
it. The honest state is unchanged from
[0005](0005-first-end-to-end-decision-pass.md): the machine works, and the
question needs weeks of data.

It also says nothing about the ~19% of successful migrations CryptoHouse has no
transfer rows for beyond "the balances covered them here". That gap is now
counted under its own skip reason, `GraduationSubjectUnresolved`, so if it grows
it will be visible instead of being filed alongside a million skipped trades.
